13.2 URINARY SYSTEM

STRUCTURES AND THEIR FUNCTIONS
- Kidney: makes urine; removes urea (nitrogenous waste / uric acid) - reject "removes urine"; removes excess salts / minerals / ions; removes toxins / poisons / hormones (reject "harmful" substances); removes water in excess (osmoregulation / varying amounts of water); by filtration of / removal from the blood / plasma; this is excretion.
- Ureter: carries urine from the kidney; to the bladder. Its wall shows peristalsis - wave-like / rhythmic; contraction of muscles (if named they must be circular); which pushes the urine along (reject "urea" alone); to the bladder.
- Bladder (urinary bladder; reject gall bladder): stores / holds / collects urine before it is released.
- Urethra: carries urine; from the bladder; to the outside of the body. In the male it also carries semen / sperm / seminal fluid (through the penis).
- Ureter and urethra compared: both carry urine (urea); ureter from kidney to bladder; urethra from bladder out of the body; urethra carries semen / sperm in the male. Similarity between the sexes: the urethra carries urine in both (reject just "urea"); difference: it carries semen / sperm / gametes only in the male.
- The spellings ureter and urethra must be correct to be credited.
- Renal artery: brings blood containing urea / waste products to the kidney - oxygenated. Renal vein: takes blood away from the kidney - deoxygenated, with the lowest urea concentration.
- Matching: renal artery - oxygenated blood; renal vein - deoxygenated blood; ureter - urine to the bladder; urethra - urine from the bladder.
- The route of urine: kidney -> ureter -> bladder -> urethra. The fluid is urine (reject urea).
- In a diagram: the renal artery comes from the aorta, the renal vein joins the vena cava, the ureter runs down from each kidney to the bladder, and the single urethra leaves the bladder.
- In the male: ureter carries urine only; urethra carries urine and sperm; sperm duct (vas deferens) carries sperm only; rectum carries neither. An enlarged prostate gland closes / restricts the urethra and so hinders urination (prevents the exit of urine from the bladder); ignore references to the bladder or to pain.
- The kidney is an organ; the organ that contains many nephrons.
- Volume of liquid passing per day: the renal vein carries slightly less than the renal artery but much more than the ureter.

WHY UREA MUST BE REMOVED
- Urea is toxic / a toxin.
- The liquid passing out of the urethra is urine and the waste product it contains is urea (accept ammonia / excess water / excess minerals).
- Most nitrogen compounds are excreted by the kidneys. People who need dialysis have kidneys that are not functioning correctly.

THE NEPHRON
- Glomerulus: the knot of capillaries that filters the blood (the capillary structures that filter urea from the blood).
- Bowman's capsule: the cup-shaped start of the nephron around the glomerulus.
- Tubule; the hairpin loop is the loop of Henle; surrounded by blood capillaries.
- Collecting duct: the final tube, which nephrons join and from which urine leaves.

FILTRATION
- Water, glucose, urea and ions (also amino acids) pass out of the blood into the Bowman's capsule / enter the nephron; by filtration (filtered from the blood).
- Protein does not enter the Bowman's capsule / the nephron / is not filtered / is not removed from the blood; because protein molecules are large. Blood cells and platelets also stay in the blood.
- Urea is lower in the renal vein than in the renal artery because: urea passes into / is removed by the kidney; by filtration; in the nephron / Bowman's capsule / glomerulus; and is excreted / passes out in the urine.

REABSORPTION
- All the glucose is reabsorbed / absorbed back into the blood (ignore "taken back into the blood"); selectively / by active transport ("selective reabsorption" earns both marks). Glucose concentration in the tubule falls to zero in its first part.
- Amino acids are also totally reabsorbed.
- Some ions are reabsorbed.
- Most of the water is reabsorbed - the volume of water in the tubule falls along the nephron (about 99 % of the water entering the kidneys is reabsorbed).
- Urea is not all reabsorbed - most stays in the tubule.
- Percentage reabsorbed = (amount entering in the blood - amount leaving in the urine) / amount entering x 100; e.g. water 150 dm3 to 1.5 dm3 = 99 %; glucose 150 g to 0 g = 100 %; urea 50 g to 30 g = 40 %.

URINE
- Urine contains: water; urea (accept nitrogenous waste or other named, e.g. uric acid, creatinine); salts / ions (accept named, e.g. sodium, chloride, ammonium, calcium). Also accepted: hormones, pigments, enzymes, toxins / drugs.
- Urine of a healthy person contains no glucose (reject sugar / glucose), no protein, no amino acids and no blood cells.
- Water in urine is important as a solvent - it dissolves urea / salts.
- Blood (renal artery) compared with urine (ureter): blood has cells / platelets / plasma, protein / antibodies / amino acids / fats, glucose, and more hormones / vitamins; urine has none of these; urine has a higher urea concentration, more toxins and more salts / ions / water. Ignore oxygen / carbon dioxide (older scheme credits them) and "minerals".
- Urine compared with blood plasma from a data table: less urea in plasma; more sodium in plasma; less chloride in plasma; quote comparative data with units; manipulate the data (e.g. urea 50 times higher in urine).
- Renal artery compared with renal vein and ureter from a data table: amino acids and protein the same in artery and vein; glucose the same or slightly higher in the artery; mineral ions and urea higher in the artery, by up to the amount that appears in the urine.
- Renal vein compared with renal artery: less urea, less oxygen, more carbon dioxide, less water (and fewer salts).

VOLUME AND CONCENTRATION OF URINE
- Hot day / more sweating: water is lost as sweat; a lower volume of / less urine; with less water / more concentrated; the kidneys reabsorb more water; to keep the blood concentration constant; fewer salts / nitrogenous compounds remain to be removed in urine because some leave in sweat. Reverse for a cold day (less water reabsorbed, more urine).
- After drinking a large volume of water: water is absorbed into the blood; the rise / excess in the water content of the blood is detected (accept hypothalamus / a correct reference to ADH); more water / a higher volume of water is excreted - more urine is produced; by the kidney (nephron); the ion concentration of the urine decreases; this is homeostasis - it keeps the concentration of water in the blood constant; urine passes via the ureter, bladder and urethra. The water potential of blood in the renal arteries and of urine in the ureters rises, with little change in the renal veins.
- Organ that makes absorbed water become water in urine: the kidney; it filters / removes water from the blood.
- Diet: more protein (reverse argument accepted); means more amino acids; broken down in / converted by the liver (deamination); so more urea in the urine / urine more concentrated; less water or more salts / ions in the diet also make the urine more concentrated. Glucose / sugar is credited only with reference to diabetes; ignore specific foods.
- A drink that produces the most urine should be avoided on a very hot day (marked independently of the drink named): it increases the volume of / water in urine; water is already being lost in sweat; more than usual; sweating is needed for temperature regulation / to keep cool; danger of dehydration / increased thirst. Ignore heat loss in urine.
- A meat-only diet on a hot day of energetic work: more urea in the urine; water lost in sweat; blood concentration has to be maintained; more water reabsorbed in the kidneys / less water in the urine / urine more concentrated.
- Investigation of urea in urine: mean = total / number of results, given to the stated significant figures (12.8 / 6 = 2.133 = 2.1). The group with the higher mean ate more protein; so more amino acids were deaminated to produce urea; so more urea was filtered / passed into the urine in the kidney. Both groups should drink the same volume of water: the volume drunk affects the volume of water used to form urine; and therefore the concentration of urea in the urine.
- A low mass of urea excreted could be due to low protein in the diet, kidneys functioning incorrectly or a liver functioning incorrectly - not to low salts in the diet.
- Kidney disease: urea in the sweat is higher; because filtration / removal of urea by the kidney is reduced; so more urea remains in the blood plasma.
- How the kidneys keep the blood at a constant concentration and why it matters: homeostasis / osmoregulation; salts / ions / urea; and water; are removed from the blood; with reabsorption into the blood; in varying quantities / of the excess; depending on food and water intake; and on loss in sweating; tissue fluid is therefore kept at a constant concentration; otherwise water would enter or leave cells; by osmosis; cells would swell / burst / shrink; enzyme action / metabolism would be affected.

THE LIVER: ASSIMILATION OF AMINO ACIDS AND DEAMINATION
- The liver is the organ that metabolises amino acids.
- Amino acids are made into proteins (e.g. plasma proteins / a named protein).
- An example of assimilation is the building of proteins from amino acids (absorption of glycerol, breakdown of glycogen or alcohol in the liver and release of a hormone are not assimilation).
- Alcohol, drugs and hormones such as progesterone are all broken down by the liver.
- Excess amino acids are broken down - deamination (or described: removal of the nitrogen-containing part).
- Forming urea; and a carbohydrate (glucose), which can be respired (lost as carbon dioxide) or stored as glycogen.
- Urea is formed from amino acids, in the liver; it is produced in the liver, filtered from the blood by the kidney, and stored (in urine) in the bladder.
- Other liver functions credited: converts glucose to glycogen and back; stores glycogen (insoluble, so no effect on the water potential of cells); breaks down / removes alcohol, drugs, toxins, lactic acid; breaks down hormones (preventing their continued effect); stores iron and vitamins; breaks down haemoglobin / red blood cells; makes bile (emulsifies lipids, increasing the surface area for lipase); high metabolic rate - heat production. The liver does not convert glucose to amino acids.
- Hormones, once they have acted, are destroyed / broken down; by the liver; and the products are removed (excreted by the kidneys).
- Blood that has passed through the liver: carbon dioxide increased, glucose increased, oxygen reduced, urea increased. In liver capillaries oxygen and amino acids pass out to the cells; carbon dioxide and urea pass into the blood.
- The liver is a major region for the assimilation of amino acids (to be identified on a diagram of the digestive system).

PATHWAYS
- Urea from the liver to the outside: transported in the blood / plasma; dissolved; hepatic vein to vena cava / heart; (lungs and heart again); renal artery; to the kidney; removed / filtered from the blood; becomes part of the urine; ureter; bladder; urethra.
- Protein eaten to urea excreted: digested / broken down / hydrolysed; into amino acids; by protease (or a named protease); absorbed in the small intestine (duodenum / ileum); into villi / capillaries / blood; hepatic portal vein; to the liver; deamination; urea carried in the renal artery; to the kidney; blood filtered / urea leaves the blood; forms urine; bladder / urethra.
- In a flow chart: protein / polypeptide -> (protease) -> amino acids -> liver -> urea (reject urine); carbohydrate stored in the liver = glycogen.
- An amino acid in the liver until its elements leave the body: made into protein; or deaminated; carbohydrate / glucose produced; respired - lost as carbon dioxide; or stored as glycogen; urea; hepatic vein; renal artery; kidney; ureter; urine (or sweat); bladder / urethra.
- Highest urea in the blood: hepatic vein; lowest: renal vein; urea is most concentrated in the ureter (urine).

REMOVAL OF EXCRETORY PRODUCTS - SUMMARY ANSWERS
- Toxic / poisonous materials (reject "harmful"); waste products of metabolism; urea; made in the liver, carried in the blood to the kidneys; leaves in urine; via the ureter, bladder and urethra (urination); water / salts / urea also leave in sweat; from sweat glands in the skin, via sweat ducts / pores, the materials coming from the blood capillaries; carbon dioxide (and water); from cells / blood to the alveoli by diffusion from the capillaries; exhaled / breathed out.
- Capillaries in excretory organs: thin walls / one cell thick; allow diffusion; short distance / close to the cells; of urea / salts into the kidney tubule; of carbon dioxide into the alveoli; and bring oxygen to the excretory tissues.
- Urine added to faeces makes a better fertiliser: a larger range of / more ions; more water; urea / nitrogenous compounds; which are easier / quicker to convert to nitrate.
