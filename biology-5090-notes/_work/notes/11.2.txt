11.2 HEART

STRUCTURE OF THE HEART
- Four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle (accept auricle for atrium); the heart has two sides / is completely divided by the septum.
- The septum separates oxygenated blood (left side) from deoxygenated blood (right side).
- Most of the heart is muscle tissue; the muscular wall is supplied with oxygen by the coronary arteries (coronary + artery both needed) - the coronary arteries carry blood to the heart muscle.
- Vessels: vena cava brings blood from the body into the right atrium; pulmonary artery carries blood from the right ventricle to the lungs; pulmonary vein brings blood from the lungs into the left atrium (the vein that carries oxygenated blood); aorta carries blood from the left ventricle to the body.
- Oxygenated blood is in the left atrium and left ventricle (and aorta and pulmonary vein).
- Atrioventricular valves lie between each atrium and ventricle: tricuspid on the right, bicuspid (mitral) on the left. Semilunar valves lie at the base of the aorta and of the pulmonary artery (the aortic valve is a semilunar valve).
- Drawing valves on a heart section: two sets of semilunar (aortic / pulmonary) valves shown; two sets of atrioventricular valves in the correct places; free ends of the semilunar valves point upwards (into the arteries); free ends of the atrioventricular valves point downwards (into the ventricles); valves correctly labelled.
- Human heart compared with fish heart: human 4 chambers, fish 2; human 4 valves, fish 2; human 4 blood vessels, fish 2; human chambers of different sizes, fish chambers of similar size.
- Arrows for blood flow: correctly into, through and out of the right side; correctly into, through and out of the left side.

PATH OF BLOOD THROUGH THE HEART
- Route: right atrium -> right ventricle -> lungs -> left atrium -> left ventricle (-> body).
- Blood returning from the body passes through the right atrium and then the right ventricle; blood in an artery to a body organ (e.g. the renal artery) last passed through the left ventricle.
- Blood passes through four chambers in each complete circuit (eight in two circuits).
- Substances absorbed in the lungs (nicotine, an inhaled drug) reach the left atrium first; nutrients absorbed from the small intestine reach the right atrium first.
- Narrowing of the entrance to the vessel leaving the right ventricle first reduces blood flow to the lungs.

RELATIVE THICKNESS OF THE WALLS
- The left ventricle has the thickest (muscular) wall.
- Ventricles are thicker-walled / more muscular than atria (e.g. left ventricle 15 mm, right ventricle 9 mm, each atrium 3 mm) - a ventricle must exert a greater force than an atrium.
- The left ventricle is thicker-walled / more muscular than the right ventricle (ORA).
- Because it has further to pump the blood (round the body) / it generates a higher pressure; greater contraction / force applied to the blood.
- Pressure in the pulmonary artery is lower than in the aorta: right ventricle wall thinner / left ventricle wall thicker (less / more muscle; weaker / stronger contraction); the pulmonary route is a shorter distance (only to the lungs); little work to do against gravity; low pressure avoids damage to the lung capillaries; the body circulation needs high pressure for filtration in the kidneys and to send oxygen / glucose to the brain.
- Evidence in a diagram that blood leaves at different pressures for the lungs and the body: the left ventricle wall is more muscular than the right ventricle wall.

HOW THE HEART IS ADAPTED AS THE PUMP IN A DOUBLE CIRCULATION
- Four chambers / has a septum.
- Blood on the left and right sides is kept separate - deoxygenated and oxygenated blood kept separate.
- Right atrium receives blood from the body; right ventricle pumps blood to the lungs.
- Left atrium receives blood from the lungs; left ventricle pumps blood to the (rest of the) body.
- Larger / thicker wall of the left ventricle (ORA) - to pump blood all round the body.
- Valves give one-way flow / prevent backflow.
- It beats continually / rhythmically; it never tires or suffers from cramp.

CARDIAC MUSCLE CONTRACTION AND VALVE ACTION
- The walls are muscular; the muscle contracts (reject "pushing / forcing / pumping" where the question already says the heart pumps); this pumps / pushes / squeezes blood and creates pressure in the blood system.
- Correct reference to the atria and to the ventricles: the two atria contract together, then the two ventricles contract together, then there is a pause.
- Atrioventricular valves open to let blood pass from atrium to ventricle.
- When the ventricle contracts the atrioventricular valve closes - to prevent the return / backflow of blood into the atrium.
- Tendons / cords hold the atrioventricular valves (reject "heartstrings") - a statement of their action is needed.
- Semilunar valves open as blood is pumped out of the ventricles into the arteries, and close when the ventricle relaxes - preventing backflow into the ventricle.
- The cycle is repeated / the actions are co-ordinated.
- Right side in order: right atrium contracts; right ventricle contracts; tricuspid valve closes; semilunar valves open (as pressure builds up). Credit: 3 marks all correct; 2 for the correct sequence started in the wrong place; 1 for any two in sequence.
- Left ventricle: (muscle) pumps / contracts / pushes blood; at high pressure; to the body / into the aorta.
- Right atrioventricular (tricuspid) valve: opens to let blood into the right ventricle; closes when blood is pumped into the pulmonary artery / to the lungs; prevents backflow of blood; into the right atrium.
- States of the valves: ventricles contracting / emptying - atrioventricular valves closed, semilunar valves open; ventricles filling - atrioventricular valves open, semilunar valves closed.
- The heart ventricle does not have its muscles arranged antagonistically: heart muscle works on its own - it is not arranged in pairs (no flexor and extensor), no other muscle relaxes when it contracts, it is not attached to bones and it does not cause movement at a joint.

PRESSURE AND VOLUME GRAPHS OF ONE HEARTBEAT
- The atrium contracts in the short period just before the steep rise in ventricular pressure.
- The atrioventricular (bicuspid) valve closes where ventricular pressure rises above atrial pressure (start of the steep rise).
- The semilunar valve opens where ventricular pressure rises above the pressure in the aorta.
- Between those two points, and again just after contraction (ventricular pressure below aortic pressure but still above atrial pressure), all the valves are closed.
- While ventricular pressure is high (plateau) the atrioventricular valve is closed and the semilunar valve is open; the rise in aortic pressure is caused by contraction of the left ventricle.
- The ventricle is contracting while its pressure is rising / high and while the volume of blood in it is falling.
- Pressure in an artery rises when the heart / ventricle contracts / pumps and falls when it relaxes (one mark for "heart beat / pulse" alone).
- A chamber in which the pressure rises sharply after the right atrium is the right ventricle (blood flows from right atrium to right ventricle / pressure increases).
- A graph for the circulation to the body differs from one for the lungs: wider variations in pressure; pressure higher; the left ventricle wall is larger / thicker / more muscular; blood has further to travel / takes longer; more fluctuations (ignore "increasing pressure").
- Heart rate from a graph or timing bar: beats per minute = 60 / time for one beat in seconds (0.6 s per beat = 100 per minute; two beats in 1.5 s = 80 per minute).

FAULTS IN THE HEART
- Hole between the left and right sides (septum): blood from both sides mixes / oxygenated and deoxygenated blood are not separated; (some oxygenated blood flows through the pulmonary artery) more oxygen in the blood sent to the lungs; less oxygen in the blood sent to the body / less oxygen reaches the muscles; less aerobic respiration / reduced energy / muscles respire anaerobically; production / build-up of lactic acid (muscle pain and tiredness in exercise); the heart beats faster / harder / has to work harder; (blood) pressure reduced; effect on the volume of blood leaving a chamber; shortness of breath; fluid build-up.
- Semilunar valve that does not open or close fully: ventricle contracts + valve opens (not fully); contracts + less blood passes through the valve; ventricle relaxes + valve closes (not fully); relaxes + backflow of blood / blood returns to the ventricle. Effect on exercise: less able to exercise / gets tired / muscle fatigue; less oxygen / oxygenated blood; to the muscles; less aerobic respiration; less energy released; anaerobic respiration; lactic acid.
- A pump fitted to help the left ventricle: it helps blood flow / pressure; its tube passes through muscle; blood flows from the ventricle through the device to the aorta; the valve bypassed is the aortic / semilunar valve.
- Blood fed into the aorta of an isolated heart reaches the heart muscle rather than the left ventricle: the valve (aortic / semilunar if named); closes; so blood is sent through the coronary vessels / arteries (reject vein).

MONITORING THE ACTIVITY OF THE HEART
- The sound of the heartbeat is caused by the closing of the valves (accept opening and closing).
- Pulse: a pulse (beat) is felt in an artery; caused by increased pressure; one pulse beat for every heart beat - so counting the pulse gives the heart rate.
- Mean pulse rate = sum of the readings / number of readings, to the nearest beat per minute.
- Drug trial data (beta-blockers): the untreated group is the control; the treated group's heart rate drops / reduces; rapidly / with quoted figures; during the first months; then remains (more or less) constant / stays low.

EFFECT OF PHYSICAL ACTIVITY ON HEART RATE
- Pulse rate increases with exercise (slow walking and fast running both raise it); the heart beats faster and more powerfully (larger stroke volume; accept higher blood pressure). The faster circulation supplies more oxygen and glucose to the working muscles and removes more carbon dioxide (and lactic acid), so the muscle cells respire faster, release more energy and contract / work at a higher rate, and lactic acid production is delayed.
- Benefits of the changes during a physical activity such as a race: adrenaline; glycogen converted to glucose; in the liver / muscles; boosts blood glucose; faster heart beat; better / faster circulation; more oxygen to the muscles; more glucose to the muscles; faster respiration / metabolism / more energy released; faster / deeper breathing; more oxygen into the blood; better removal of carbon dioxide; sweating / vasodilation; to cool down / lose heat / maintain body temperature.
- Explanation - an enhancement word (more / faster / greater) must appear: muscles (are working); more / faster blood (flow / circulation); more oxygen / glucose supplied; more carbon dioxide / lactic acid removed; more energy released (reject produced / made / manufactured).
- A rise in pulse rate reduces the need for anaerobic respiration.
- After exercise the pulse rate begins to fall as soon as the exercise stops; recovery time is read from the graph.
- The fitter person has the lower pulse rate and the rate returns to resting (recovers) more quickly.
- Reasons two people respond differently (the person must be identified, otherwise max 2): fitter / exercises regularly; more efficient muscles / better breakdown of lactic acid; more efficient circulation / little cholesterol in the blood vessels; more efficient lungs; exercised less vigorously; more haemoglobin / red blood cells; (the other) smoker; obese / overweight (reject "large mass"); emphysema; asthma / bronchitis / heart problems / lung infection.
- Changes brought about by fitness training: larger lung capacity / stronger intercostal muscles; stronger / thicker cardiac / ventricle muscle; cardiac muscle pumps more blood in each cardiac cycle; improved blood supply to the heart muscle; more haemoglobin / red blood cells.
- At altitude breathing becomes faster and deeper ("breathe more" = one mark) and the heart beats faster and more powerfully ("more" = one mark): faster circulation of blood; supplying more oxygen / compensating for the lower oxygen concentration; removing more carbon dioxide.
- Adrenaline (excitement, fright, shock): heart rate rises sharply at once and then returns gradually to resting; blood glucose concentration rises (the liver converts glycogen to glucose).
- Nicotine is the substance in cigarette smoke mainly responsible for a rise in heart rate.
- Chest compressions and forced breathing in first aid: oxygen reaches the lungs / alveoli; oxygen passes into the blood / red blood cells / haemoglobin; by diffusion; from high to low concentration; (compression) blood is forced from the left ventricle; to the organs / tissues / cells; for aerobic respiration; may re-start the heart.

CORONARY HEART DISEASE (CHD)
- Blockage / narrowing of the coronary arteries (by fatty deposits - atheroma / cholesterol; a blood clot may complete the blockage).
- Less blood / glucose / oxygen reaches the heart muscle.
- So the heart muscle cannot contract correctly / cannot pump blood - cells in the wall of the (left) ventricle die.
- Names accepted for the condition: coronary heart disease / heart disease / CHD / atherosclerosis / arteriosclerosis / cardiac disease / angina.
- Effects on the person: heart attack / heart stops / angina / breathlessness; less ability of the heart to contract / pump blood; less blood to the body / tissues / organs (or a named one); less oxygen / glucose to the body / tissues / organs; less aerobic respiration / more anaerobic respiration; production of lactic acid; less ability to carry out physical activity / fatigue.
- By-pass surgery joins a vessel from the aorta to the coronary artery beyond the blockage.
- Material causing the blockage - two from: fat / cholesterol / blood cells / clot (clotted blood) (accept platelets; accept fibres / fibrin; atheroma earns 1 mark). Anti-platelet drugs are given before a stent is inserted: the natural response to damage / injury is for blood to clot; platelets release enzymes / cause fibrinogen to change to fibrin; so the drug prevents clotting.
- Balloon and metal mesh (stent) treatment: inflating the balloon opens the metal mesh; pushes / compresses the blockage / fat; widens the blood vessel / lumen. Leaving the mesh in place maintains the wider lumen; and increases blood flow.

RISK FACTORS FOR CHD
- Diet high in fat / oil / (saturated, animal) fat / cholesterol; high blood cholesterol.
- Obesity / being overweight.
- High blood pressure.
- Smoking.
- Lack of exercise / sedentary lifestyle (ignore "exercise" or "lifestyle" unqualified).
- Stress (accept anxiety / depression).
- Genetics / inheritance / family history - CHD has a genetic link / is affected by genes / is hereditary / passed from parents to children (accept: children share their parents' lifestyle).
- Age / old age / time factor.
- Gender / sex.
- Diabetes.
- Not causes: a diet low in salt; regular exercise.
- Points-score tables: add the points for each factor; equal totals mean an equal risk (a wrong total scores nothing).
- Death rates given per 100 000 of population, separately for males and females, cannot be explained by one country simply having more males.
- Smoking / nicotine: increased blood pressure (a graph shows a rise while the cigarette is smoked, followed by a fall); carbon monoxide is credited with a harmful effect on oxygen carriage / fatty deposits in the walls of blood vessels; artery constriction (vasoconstriction, reduced diameter); atherosclerosis / atheroma / fat deposits / increased blood fat; increased clotting tendency; increased heart rate / heart pumps harder; may lead to blockage of the coronary arteries, heart attack, stroke.
- High-fat diet: obesity; strain on the skeleton / effect on joints; strain on the heart / it pumps harder / faster; breathing difficulties; risk of diabetes; social implications; atheroma (accept cholesterol; reject deposits 'in veins' or 'on arteries'; ignore 'blood vessels'); high blood pressure; heart disease / heart attack / other cardiovascular condition.
- Being overweight: extra mass / load on the body / pressure on the joints; joint / skeletal problems; occlusion of the coronary arteries / arteries in the brain; increased risk of (coronary) heart disease / heart attacks; stroke; diabetes.

DIET AND EXERCISE IN REDUCING THE RISK
- Reduce fat in the diet (reject "no fat") - particularly animal / saturated fat.
- This reduces deposition on / in the artery walls (atheroma / atherosclerosis; the deposit may be cholesterol) - in the coronary arteries of the heart; and avoids raised blood pressure. Ignore: "cholesterol" as the dietary change; "heart attack".
- Skimmed milk is better than whole milk: less (overall) fat; particularly saturated fat; less deposition in blood vessels / atheroma / raised blood pressure / heart disease.
- Factors that decrease the risk: high intake of fruit and vegetables; low blood cholesterol; low intake of processed foods; eating more fruit (vitamin C) and less butter.
- Eating large amounts of high-fat food such as cheese: obesity; a named circulatory disorder.
- Regular exercise keeps the circulation efficient (see fitness training above).
- Percentage of the total blood supply going to one organ = supply to the organ / total supply x 100 (e.g. brain at rest 750 / 5800 x 100 = 12.9 or 13 %); working mark for the correct expression.
- Table of blood supply to organs at rest and during strenuous exercise: a missing value is found by subtracting the other organs from the total (e.g. kidneys 600 cm3 per minute during exercise, down from 1100 at rest); supply to skeletal muscle, heart and skin rises, supply to kidneys and digestive organs falls, supply to the brain is unchanged.
- A heart kept beating outside the body must be supplied with blood to which glucose and oxygen are added: for energy (reject "production" of energy); released in respiration; by the heart muscle / cells (also accepted: adrenaline to stimulate the heart muscle; insulin for glucose uptake; heparin to stop clotting).
