F. EXTRACTION AND MATCHING ISSUES (all handled; listed so that the tracker can be interpreted correctly)
   1. Every PDF page in the folder carries three content streams (a colour-inversion wrapper round the original). The wrapper was neutralised before any page was rendered.
   2. The wrapper also leaves white as the current fill colour, so anything a page draws before it sets a colour of its own is invisible unless the first wrapper stream is neutralised as well. The first renderer of the second session did not do that. A page-by-page comparison of both renderers over all 315 files found 230 affected pages, all in 16 question papers of 2003-2009. The renderer was corrected; the 84 "figure viewed" Paper 1 questions lying on those pages were inspected again with the corrected renderer and every key was confirmed. The Paper 2 figure inspections were made with the corrected renderer (two early ones on pages that the comparison showed to be unaffected).
   3. Landscape mark schemes (2013 onwards) are stored as rotated pages; text was re-ordered into display orientation before parsing.
   4. Five mark-scheme layouts occur (2004-2012 plain text; 2013; 2014-2016; 2017-2019; 2020 onwards). Each Paper 2 part was matched to its mark-scheme section by label; 2,400 mark-scheme labels were matched and none is empty. Where a mark scheme gives one section for several question parts (or the reverse) the tracker line names every label used.
   5. Either / Or questions of 2004-2010 appear as 8E / 8O; both alternatives are tracked, so papers up to 2022 total 90 marks (80 from 2023).
   6. Some question papers print the marks only after the last of several sub-parts; such parts are tracked as one line (remark "includes unmarked sibling part(s)").
   7. Mark-scheme sections may end with harmless carried-over lines (page banner, repeated table header, "Section B", and - in the 2026 files - a download watermark line). They were ignored when reading.
   8. The braille-paper appendix of 5090_w25_ms_22.pdf (pages 15-16) is not part of the standard paper and was skipped.
   9. Page 4 of 5090_s15_ms_22.pdf stores its text as overlapping fragments; it was rebuilt from character positions.
   10. Point counts in the tracker are counts of ";" in the matched mark-scheme text. They can differ from the marks: "max n" lists hold more points than marks; table-form schemes hold none. For ten parts the marks column of the mark scheme could not be read back reliably (s14_21:1(b)(i), s14_21:1(b)(ii), s14_22:1(c), w14_21:2(b)(iii), w14_22:6(a), s15_21:2(b), s15_22:2(a), w15_21:4(b), w15_22:9(b), w19_22:4(a)(iii)); the tracker marks come from the question papers and add up to the paper totals for all 78 papers.
   11. Paper 1 option tables are sometimes split across text rows or drawn as ticks and crosses; where the extracted options were not unambiguous the page image was viewed (status "figure viewed").
   12. The page number shown for a Paper 2 part is the page on which its text starts; its figure can be on the neighbouring page (those pages were opened as well during the figure inspection).
   13. Unofficial "solved" scans and errata sheets in the folder were not used as mark schemes.

G. LIMITS OF THIS VERIFICATION (read before relying on the notes)
   1. One verifier worked through the papers sequentially. The environment offered no parallel workers, so the coordinator / worker scheme of the brief could not be used and there was no second independent reader.
   2. The working state was lost once when the work environment was reset. It was rebuilt from the tracker and notes that had been saved. All question and mark-scheme text was then extracted again from the PDFs, but the 2,611 "verified" and 2,902 "out of scope" decisions of the first pass were restored from that tracker and were not re-read one by one. They were re-tested indirectly by the checks listed in the final report (key re-extraction, complete re-read of every out-of-scope Paper 2 mark scheme, vocabulary re-check of out-of-scope Paper 1 questions, lexical coverage checks, figure re-inspections).
   3. Out-of-scope Paper 1 questions (1,654) were classified from stem, options and key. Their figures were not rendered, so their keys were not checked against the figures; nothing from them is in the notes.
   4. Out-of-scope Paper 2 parts (1,040): the whole mark-scheme text of each was read; their figures were not rendered.
   5. In-scope Paper 2 parts: the figure was inspected for the 159 parts whose marking points name or mark structures on a figure. For the other parts with a figure (status "text sufficient / figure not rendered") graphs and data tables were not rendered; their readings were taken from the mark scheme, and points that depend entirely on one graph or table are summarised as method rules.
   6. A bullet line in the notes merges the wording of every mark scheme that credits the same idea, and one line often holds several ";" points. The number of bullet lines is therefore much smaller than the number of marking points read, and is not a count of marks.
   7. The lexical coverage check compares words, not meaning. It shows that a marking point is represented in the notes of its subsection; it cannot prove that the wording is equivalent. Points below the threshold were read by hand.
   8. The syllabus allocation follows the list supplied in the brief (2026-2028 numbering). Where two sections touch, the choices in section H were made; a different reader could draw some of these lines differently.

H. SCOPE BOUNDARY RULES (how points that touch an unrequested section were treated)
   The requested list was used exactly as supplied. An unofficial transcription of the learning objectives of the same syllabus, found in a public repository (see _work/ref/README.txt), was used only to decide to which section a point belongs where two sections touch; nothing was added to the list from it.
   1. A question whose subject is a requested subsection is in scope with all its marking points, including older-syllabus points on the same subject.
   2. A question whose subject is an unrequested section is out of scope. It gets a mixed code only when its mark scheme (Paper 2) or its keyed answer (Paper 1) rests on a point that is itself a statement of a requested subsection - a requested concept applied in another context.
   3. The main neighbours:
      - Nuclear division, chromosome number, haploid / diploid, asexual v sexual reproduction (sections 16.1, 16.2): out of scope in both papers. Kept in 17.3: meiosis, random fertilisation and sexual reproduction as sources of genetic variation; sex chromosomes in gametes; chromosome mutation (Down's syndrome, triploidy); points credited inside inheritance questions. A block of nuclear-division points that an earlier step of this work had placed in 17.3 was taken out on this ground; it is kept for reference in _work/removed/17.3_nuclear_division_block.txt.
