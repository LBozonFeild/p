MITOSIS, MEIOSIS, FERTILISATION AND CHROMOSOME NUMBER (points credited in reproduction questions)
- Mitosis (max 2): cell / nuclear division; producing genetically identical cells / nuclei; maintaining the chromosome number. Where it occurs: in body cells or named cells (accept in the zygote); it produces two cells that are copies / identical (one scheme also accepts "similar"); ignore "growth / repair within a cell".
- Roles of mitosis: growth; repair / replacement of cells / tissues; asexual / vegetative reproduction / cloning. A zygote divides by mitosis.
- Mitosis v meiosis (points must be comparative; each point must be linked to the right division): chromosome number maintained (diploid to diploid; 46 to 46) v halved (diploid to haploid; 46 to 23; reduction division); 2 new (daughter) cells v 4; identical offspring / clones / no genetic variation v genetic variation in the offspring; occurs in all organs / body cells (plant, animal, bacteria) v only in gamete-producing organs (gonads: testes, ovaries, anthers); for growth / repair / replacement / asexual reproduction (single parent) v to produce gametes / sex cells (any gamete correctly named) / sexual reproduction (ignore "fertilisation"). Meiosis occurs in ovaries / ovules and testes / anthers ("gonads" earns one mark only). Reject growth / repair for meiosis.
- Meiosis in sexual reproduction / gamete formation: cell division; reference to chromosomes; in the testes / anthers / ovaries; diploid to haploid / halving of the chromosome number; gamete formation / named gametes; all gametes genetically different; so that fertilisation restores the (diploid) chromosome number; leads to variation in the population.
- How the cells of offspring have the same chromosome number as the cells of their parents (max 6): in sexual reproduction; gametes; produced by reduction division / meiosis; have half the number of chromosomes / are haploid; fertilisation; fusion of nuclei / gametes; restoration of the chromosome number / diploid; in asexual reproduction; cells divide by mitosis.
- Gamete nucleus (sperm, pollen grain, ovule's female gamete) compared with a body-cell nucleus (cheek cell, cell of the adult plant): haploid v diploid / half the number / fewer / a different number of chromosomes (one scheme accepted a stated number, another ignored any stated numbers); half the number of genes / amount of DNA; either X or Y - only one sex chromosome; difference in shape / size; made by meiosis, the body cell by mitosis (the reference must be to how the cell is produced). The gamete is the male / female contribution to reproduction; the body cell is part of the adult and not involved in reproduction. An ovule holds a haploid female gamete; the seed formed after fertilisation is diploid.
- Why the gamete nucleus must differ: fusion / fertilisation; with another gamete / ovum / egg nucleus; forms a zygote; restores the diploid / full number. Also credited: sperm nuclei differ from one another and from body-cell nuclei because meiosis gives different allele combinations on the chromosomes.
- Number of chromosomes in a gamete and in a body cell (max 4): a gamete (sex cell; one named gamete accepted) has half the number of a somatic / body cell - one set versus two sets; correct use of haploid + diploid; 46 v 23; gametes / haploid cells are the result of meiosis / reduction division; somatic cells are produced by mitosis; the diploid number is restored at fertilisation; each parent has an equal share in the genotype of the offspring.
- Sperm production: in the testes; by meiosis / reduction division; chromosome number halved; haploid.
- A cell from a growing region of a plant whose diploid number is 24 has 24 chromosomes: the cells there divide by mitosis (accept production of genetically identical cells / the cells are not gametes). The chromosomes of a diploid cell are in its nucleus.
- The ovary produces haploid ova / eggs (as well as the hormones oestrogen and progesterone).
- Asexual reproduction (definition, any two): production of genetically identical offspring / clones; by mitosis; from one parent (no gametes required). It gives genetically identical offspring because there is one parent; and the same / no new combination of genes / alleles; it involves mitosis, not meiosis; there are no seeds / no sexual reproduction (sexual reproduction / gametes produce genetic variation). Credited consequences: offspring have the same properties as the parent / are genetically identical / a clone (reject "similar", accept "same"); the required / known / desired characteristic is ensured; no variation; no danger of inbreeding or of introducing undesirable traits; all the cells of a clone can make the same product (e.g. bacteria dividing by binary fission all make insulin). Disadvantages credited: the plants all share the same undesirable feature / allele; they are not adapted if the environment changes.
- A seedling grown from a seed is not genetically identical to its parent plant: sexual reproduction (shown by the remains of a flower); self- or cross-pollination; with some genes / chromosomes from another flower / plant.
- A seed and the fruit wall round it are genetically different: the seed results from fertilisation / sexual reproduction; fusion of gametes (pollen nucleus + egg); gametes often from different plants / flowers; gametes made by meiosis / reduction division; giving a new combination of (different) genes / alleles (max 3 for the seed); the fruit wall results from mitosis; so is genetically identical to the parent / a clone.
- Importance of cell division in germination: mitosis; produces genetically identical cells / clones; growth / new protoplasm / new organelles; of the radicle and plumule.
- Three sets of chromosomes in each cell (triploid, 3n) instead of two: a disadvantage is interference with sexual reproduction / infertility - meiosis (reduction division) is not possible, so gamete formation / seed production is impaired. It is commoner in plants than in animals because many plants can reproduce asexually / propagate vegetatively - mitosis is unaffected / meiosis is not involved (also credited: triploids are more hardy / survive better).
