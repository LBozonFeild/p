# 7 Transport in flowering plants

## 7.1.1 Root hair — structure ↔ water + ion uptake
**Credit (pair them):**
- long / extension / projection / finger-like → large **SA**
- large SA → absorb **water and ions**
- **thin** wall → short distance / easy entry
- cell **membrane** → water **osmosis/diffusion**; ions **active transport**
- membrane **partially/selectively permeable** → osmosis
- living / mitochondria → **energy/ATP** from **respiration** (ions)
- vacuole → store water/ions; turgor / water potential

**Water in:** osmosis (or diffusion); **high → low water potential**; through **wall then membrane**.  
**Ions in:** **active transport**; **against** concentration gradient; energy/ATP from respiration; **carrier proteins**. Diffusion of ions only if the question is **down** a gradient. If soil [ion] < cell [ion] → **AT only**.  
If asked why ions: **nitrate → amino acids/protein**; **Mg → chlorophyll**.

**R:** water by AT; ions by osmosis; “partially permeable **wall**” (wall is fully permeable).  
Low O₂ / stop aerobic respiration → less ATP → **slower ion uptake**; water osmosis **unchanged**. Large SA ≠ proof of AT. Proof of AT = against gradient **or** slower in low O₂.

**Root hair vs villus**
- Same: elongated / large SA; absorption; AT / diffusion; ions; water.
- Diff: hair = **one cell + wall**, from **soil**. Villus = **many cells + blood vessels/lacteals**, from **gut** (glucose, amino acids, lipids).

## 7.1.2 Pathway (order locked)
**root hair (cell) → (root) cortex (cell) → xylem (vessel) → palisade/spongy mesophyll (cell)**  
Leftover marks: cell-to-cell; through **or** between walls; osmosis between cells. Do **not** replace the four names.  
Fertiliser fill-in: **C**ortex **X**ylem **M**esophyll.  
Water + mineral ions travel **together in xylem**, not phloem.  
**MCQ trap:** phloem in the path, skip cortex, mesophyll before xylem.

## 7.1.3 Stain, cut stem
Leafy shoot / cut stem in **stained water** (eosin / food dye). Dye pulled up. TS: stain in **xylem** only (inner part of stem bundle); phloem/cortex/pith unstained. Why: **transpiration pull** up the continuous xylem tube.  
Cut shoot **under water** (no air lock). Potted plant: **bag the pot** so mass loss = leaf transpiration not soil evaporation.  
**Potometer** = water **uptake** ≈ transpiration (some water used in photosynthesis). Rate = Δmass or (bubble distance × capillary area) ÷ time. Change **one** factor. Fan on → graph **steeper**. Humidity graph: **highest at low humidity, lowest at high**.

## 7.2.1 Definition
**Loss of water vapour from leaves** (through **stomata**). Also credit: evaporation at **mesophyll cell surfaces** then loss through stomata. Older: “aerial parts” still accepted.  
**R:** evaporation from **epidermis** as the main cause of the pull; transpiration ≠ translocation ≠ AT ≠ using water in photosynthesis.

## 7.2.2 Out of the leaf
1 evaporation  
2 from **surface / cell wall / film of water** of **mesophyll cells**  
3 water **vapour**  
4 **air / intercellular spaces**  
5 **diffusion** / high → low concentration / down gradient  
6 through **stomata**

If they ask **name + define** (arrows):  
- cell → cell: **osmosis** = water from higher → lower water potential across a **partially permeable membrane**  
- liquid on wall → vapour: **evaporation** = liquid → gas/vapour  
- vapour → outside: **diffusion** = net movement high → lower concentration  

MCQ xylem→mesophyll / wall→air space / stoma→air = **osmosis → evaporation → diffusion**.

## 7.2.3(a) Rate — factor + direction + why
**Wind ↑ → rate ↑:** vapour blown away from leaf; humidity around leaf falls; **steeper diffusion/concentration gradient**; faster diffusion.  
**Temperature ↑ → rate ↑:** more **KE** / molecules faster; faster **evaporation**.  
**Humidity ↑ → rate ↓:** smaller vapour-concentration difference leaf vs air; **shallower gradient**; slower diffusion. **R guard cells/stomata open/close** as the humidity explanation.  
**Light ↑ → rate ↑:** **stomata open / open wider**; larger area for vapour to escape.

Highest combo: **wind + warm + dry (low humidity) + light/day**. 100% humidity, fog/rain, night (stomata closed) → lowest. Same T, light vs dark: mass falls **faster in light**.

**Same weather, different rates:** leaf **SA** / number or size of leaves; stomata **number / density / size**; stomata **upper vs lower** surface; **cuticle** thickness; **sunken stomata / hairs / rolled leaves** (trap humid air → lower rate); root system / number of root hairs (supply).

## 7.2.3(b) Wilting
Water **lost** (transpiration/evaporation/stomata) **faster than gained** / loss **>** uptake → cells **lose turgor / become flaccid** → no longer **support** the plant. **Prefer flaccid/lose turgor** (plasmolysed was **R** on an older wilting MS).  
Causes: dry soil; damaged/removed **root hairs**; blocked **xylem**; heat; low humidity; wind; high light.

**Transplant:** root hairs damaged → less water absorbed → wilt; hairs **regrow** → water in → cells **turgid** again.  
**Blocked xylem:** water from leaves cannot be **replaced** → lose turgor → wilt; **Mg** (etc.) not to leaves → **less chlorophyll** → yellow; less photosynthesis → less sugar/protein → poor growth/fruit.  
**Cuttings:** remove leaves → less **SA/stomata** → less transpiration; transparent bag → **higher humidity** → smaller gradient → less loss.  
Mature vs seedling: intact roots/hairs **and lignified xylem** so wilts less.  
Wilting is **not** “more absorbed than lost”.

## 7.2.5 Up the xylem (recent only — drop root pressure / capillarity as the answer)
1 water **evaporates/transpires from leaves**  
2 **lowers water potential of leaves**  
3 water up **xylem vessels**  
4 **transpiration pull / stream**  
5 **forces of attraction between water molecules** / column of water held together  
6 prevents loss of turgidity / wilting  

**Main cause MCQ:** evaporation from **walls of mesophyll cells**. Not AT, not epidermis, not photosynthesis using water. **Xylem water movement does not use energy.**

## 7.2.6 Translocation
Movement of **sucrose and amino acids** in **phloem** from **sources** (produce or **release**) to **sinks** (use or **store**); in **solution**; **bidirectional** (xylem is up only). Glucose from photosynthesis → converted to **sucrose** then into phloem.  
Source: leaf; storage organ **releasing** (germinating seed/tuber). Sink: **root**, meristem/shoot, **fruit/seed**, storage **storing**.  
**R glucose / food / starch / mineral ions** as phloem’s job.

**Aphids:** pierce **phloem** — sucrose + amino acids in solution; **not lignified / softer**. Many aphids → less sucrose (less respiration/energy) + less amino acids (less protein/cytoplasm) → impaired growth.  
**Mistletoe:** roots into host **phloem** to take **sucrose** (little chlorophyll of its own).  
**Cut flowers in sucrose:** no photosynthesis → no glucose/sucrose made; sucrose in **phloem** → **respiration/energy** → last longer.

## 7.2.7 TS non-woody dicot — xylem, phloem, cortex only
**Root:** xylem **centre** (often star/X); phloem **between arms / around xylem** (not a ring of bundles); wide **cortex** (epidermis → vascular tissue).  
**Stem:** **vascular bundles in a ring**, towards the **outside**; each bundle **xylem inside** (towards pith), **phloem outside** (towards epidermis); cortex between epidermis and the ring.  
Draw: ≥3 oval bundles near outside; each split in two; label phloem on the **outer** side.

## 7.2.8–9 Xylem
Functions only: transport **water + mineral ions**; **support**. **Not sugars.**

| Function | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| transport water and/or minerals | long/thin; **no end/cross walls**; **hollow**; **no cytoplasm/contents** |
| support / stem upright | **lignified** / woody / thickened / hard walls |

**(a)** thick walls + **lignin** → support / stop collapse (under tension); (extra) waterproof so column held. Details of lignification **not required**.  
**(b)** no cell contents → dead/hollow → free flow / less resistance.  
**(c)** joined end-to-end, **no cross walls** → long **continuous tube** → uninterrupted column / pull can act.  
Young cell → xylem: elongate; lose contents/die/hollow; lose end walls; add lignin.  
Xylem = **dead** (no cytoplasm/nucleus/membrane as a living cell). Root hair = **living**.

## Transpiration necessary vs problem
Need: water to leaves for **photosynthesis**; **ions** roots → leaves; **turgor/support**; **cools** leaf.  
Problem: too much loss → **wilt/death**; close stomata to stop transpiration → less CO₂ → **slower photosynthesis**.

**Guard cells:** control stomatal size; water loss/transpiration; also O₂/CO₂ for photosynthesis and respiration.

**Ions in xylem:** dissolve in water; carried by transpiration stream; e.g. nitrate → amino acids (then amino acids can move in **phloem**); Mg → chlorophyll in palisade.

**Essay soil → air:** soil → root hair **osmosis** (high→low WP, through membrane) → cortex → xylem → **transpiration pull** + **attraction between water molecules** → mesophyll → evaporate from mesophyll surfaces into **air spaces** as vapour → **diffuse** out **stomata**.

# 7 MCQ traps
1 Water = osmosis; ions = AT. Never swap.  
2 AT needs O₂/respiration. Nitrate graph: uptake **continues after** external [ion] = internal [ion].  
3 Water up xylem **not** AT / not energy-consuming.  
4 Pull starts at **mesophyll walls**, not epidermis, not photosynthesis using water.  
5 Path: hair → cortex → **xylem** → mesophyll. Phloem is the trap.  
6 Phloem = **sucrose + amino acids**, not glucose/starch/ions.  
7 Xylem = water + ions + support. Not sugars.  
8 Stem: phloem **outer**, xylem **inner**. Root: xylem **central**.  
9 Highest rate: windy, warm, **dry**, light. 100% humidity kills rate even if hot.  
10 Wilt = loss **>** uptake → flaccid. Reverse option is there on purpose.  
11 Stained cut stem → dye in **xylem**.  
12 Root hair living; xylem vessel dead.  
13 Vapour leaving leaf = **diffusion**, not translocation / osmosis / AT.  
14 Light ↑ transpiration because **stomata open**.  
15 Cuticle / stomatal density / leaf SA change rate in identical weather.

# 13 Excretion

## 13.1.1 Definition
**Removal** from the **organism/body** of **toxic/poisonous** materials **and waste products of metabolism**.  
**R harmful** (too vague); **R salts** as the definition. Must-have: removal + toxic + metabolic waste.

**Not excretion:** **egestion** = undigested food / cellulose / fibre / lignin / roughage (**not** from metabolism); hormone **secretion** into blood; **sweat** for cooling; faeces from anus.  
Example MCQ of excretion: **CO₂ removed from the lungs**. Not faeces, not sweat, not hormones.

## 13.1.2 CO₂
Waste of **(aerobic) respiration**; excreted through **lungs**; **diffusion** blood → alveoli; **exhaled**.

## 13.1.3 Urea
**Toxic** waste **produced in the liver** from **breakdown of excess amino acids**.  
Made in **liver, never kidney**. Kidney **filters/removes** it. Production-site MCQ = **liver**.

## 13.2.1 Organs (spelling marked: ureter ≠ urethra)
| | function |
|---|---|
| **kidney** | remove **urea + excess salts + water** from **blood** as **urine** / filter blood |
| **ureter** | **urine** kidney → bladder |
| **bladder** | **store/collect** urine (does not filter) |
| **urethra** | urine bladder → **out**. **R “just urea”**. Male: also **semen/sperm** |

**Ureter vs urethra:** both carry urine; ureter kidney→bladder; urethra bladder→out; male urethra also sperm.

**Renal artery:** blood **to** kidney (urea in). **Renal vein:** blood **away**, **less urea**. Also: renal vein **less O₂, more CO₂** (kidney respired), usually **less water**.  
s23/11 renal vein vs artery: **more CO₂, less urea, less water**.

**Blood vs urine:** blood has cells, plasma **protein**, **glucose**, lower urea. Healthy urine: **no cells, no protein, no glucose**, **higher urea**, excess water/ions.

## 13.2.2 Need
Urea is **toxic / a toxin** if it accumulates. That is the whole LO.

## 13.2.3 Nephron structure (name only)
**Glomerulus** (capillary knot); **Bowman’s capsule** (cup around it); **tubules**; **loop of Henle**; **collecting duct**; blood in / blood out.  
s24/22: P = Bowman’s capsule; Q = loop of Henle; R = collecting duct. Filter = **glomerulus**.  
**Do not** write podocytes, basement membrane, vasa recta, countercurrent, afferent/efferent pressure. Details **not required**.

## 13.2.4 Nephron function (exact three bullets)
**(a)** glomerulus **filters** **water, glucose, urea and ions** from blood into Bowman’s / nephron.  
**(b)** nephron **reabsorbs all glucose, some ions, most water** back into blood.  
**(c)** urine contains **urea + excess water + excess ions**.

**Glucose 0 in urine:** enters Bowman’s by **filtration**, then **all reabsorbed**.  
**Protein 0 in urine:** **too large** → **not filtered** / does not enter Bowman’s. Different reasons.  
If a table shows amino acids in filtrate then 0 in urine: **totally reabsorbed** (like glucose). Ions in urine can be **higher** than in blood (excess excreted).

Pattern (learn %, not the grams): water ~**99%** reabsorbed; glucose **100%**; urea only partly (e.g. **40%**). Trap: quoting **amount** reabsorbed (148.5 dm³) as a %. Huge volume **filtered**; **most water reabsorbed** → urine volume ≪ filtrate. Over a day ureter volume ≈ urethra volume (same urine).

Healthy, normal diet — urine has urea, salts, water. **Not glucose.** Glucose in urine → **diabetes** (not all filtered glucose reabsorbed).  
More water drunk → **higher urine volume**, **lower ion concentration**; homeostasis / keep [water] in blood constant.  
More protein eaten → more AA **deaminated** → **more urea** filtered into urine.

**Urea in vessels:** highest = **hepatic vein** (liver just made it). Lowest = **renal vein** (kidney just removed it). Hepatic portal = high glucose/AA from gut, **not** highest urea.

## 13.2.5 Assimilation
Liver: amino acids → **proteins**.  
Ileum → blood = **absorption**. Amino acids in cell → protein = **assimilation**.  
Not assimilation: starch → glucose (digestion).

## 13.2.6 Deamination
**Removal of the nitrogen-containing part (–NH₂)** of amino acids → **urea**. Not the R group, not the acid group, not the whole molecule. Kidney does **not** deaminate. No urea cycle / ammonia / keto acids needed.

Path they mark: AA absorbed in villi → **hepatic portal vein** → **liver** deaminates → urea in **renal artery** → kidney **filters** → **urine** → bladder → urethra.

**Essay urine:** blood to kidney → glomerulus **filters water, glucose, urea, ions** into Bowman’s → protein/cells stay in blood (too large) → tubules reabsorb **all glucose, some ions, most water** → collecting duct urine = **urea + excess water + excess ions** → ureter → bladder (store) → urethra (out).

# 13 MCQ traps
1 Liver **makes** urea; kidney **removes** it.  
2 Excretion ≠ egestion ≠ secretion ≠ sweating.  
3 CO₂ from respiration out of **lungs** **is** excretion.  
4 Ureter = kidney→bladder; urethra = out. Spelling counts.  
5 Urethra carries **urine**, not “urea”.  
6 Protein too big to filter; glucose **is** filtered then **all** reabsorbed. Both 0 in urine for **different** reasons.  
7 Healthy urine: urea + excess water + excess ions. No glucose/protein/cells.  
8 Glucose in urine → diabetes, not “kidney always lets glucose through”.  
9 More protein → more **urea** in urine, not more glucose.  
10 More water drunk → dilute urine (lower conc, higher volume).  
11 Renal vein: **less urea and less O₂** than renal artery.  
12 Highest urea = hepatic vein; lowest = renal vein.  
13 Deamination = remove **N-containing** bit, in **liver**.  
14 Assimilation of AA = → **proteins**, not → urea.  
15 Bladder stores; does not filter.  
16 Filtrate volume ≫ urine because **most water reabsorbed**.  
17 Not all urea is reabsorbed.  
18 Do not use skin/sweat as a syllabus excretory organ.
