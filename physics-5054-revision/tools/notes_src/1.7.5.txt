- E = P × t in any form (C1); answer (A1). P = W/t is accepted for the first mark, as is the product with unconverted units or a wrong power of ten.

== B | Power when lifting or moving a load | mgh|lift|climb|stairs|weight ×|force ×|Fv|raise|pump|height | 1
- Power when lifting: work = force × distance and power = work ÷ time (C1); power = mass × g × height ÷ time, i.e. weight × height ÷ time (A1).
- Work done = force × distance (C1); P = work ÷ time (C1); answer (A1).
- The power is larger when a load is lifted faster: the same work is done (B1) in a shorter time (B1).
- Measuring the power of a person running upstairs – readings: the mass (or weight) of the person (B1); the time taken to run up the stairs (B1); the vertical height of the stairs, or the number of steps and the height of each (B1).
- A precaution for one of the readings: the balance reads zero before the person steps on; time with two timers, or repeat and average; hold the rule vertical / the tape taut when measuring the height (B1).
* Power = weight × vertical height ÷ time = mgh ÷ t; a metre rule (height) and a stop-watch (time) are needed to measure the power output when a known load is lifted.
* For the same distance and time the largest force gives the greatest power; to compare, work out m × h ÷ t for each.
* Use the total mass or total weight lifted.
* Power of falling water = mgh per second; power output = efficiency × power input.
* For a horizontal movement, work = force × distance in the direction of the force (the weight does no work), then power = work ÷ time.
== A | Power: definition, unit and calculation (P = E ÷ t) | power|watt|P =  | 2
- Power = work done, or energy transferred, per unit time (B1). P = E ÷ t in any form (C1); answer (A1).
- The watt: a rate of transfer of energy (energy ÷ time) (C1) of 1 joule in one second (A1).
- A power rating of 100 W means that 100 J (B1) of energy is transferred (converted) every second (B1).
- Use the time in seconds (2 minutes = 120 s), reading it from the graph where necessary.
- Useful power = useful energy (work done) ÷ time (C1, A1).
- Average rate of using or releasing energy = energy ÷ time, in W or J/s (C1, A1).
- Time needed: t = E ÷ P (C1, A1).
- When only part of the energy is passed on: find that energy (C1); P = E ÷ t with the time in seconds (C1); answer (A1).
- Constant force, increasing speed: the distance moved in one second increases as the speed increases (B1), so the work done in one second – the power – increases (B1).
- Average power from repeated actions: count the number of times the action is done in the time (B1); power = total work ÷ time = work for one action × number ÷ time (B1).
- The appliance that supplies more useful energy per minute (greater useful power output) heats water to boiling first: it transfers energy at a faster rate.
* Power = work done ÷ time = energy transferred per second = rate of doing work; force × distance is work, not power.
* Unit of power: the watt, W = J/s; energy and work are measured in joules.
* Change the time to seconds before dividing (24 h = 24 × 3600 s); an answer in seconds may need changing to minutes.
