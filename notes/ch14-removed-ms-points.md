# Chapter 14 — MS points taken out of the main list

**Sources:** 5070 papers **2010–2025** (pre-2010 not used).

These are Ch 14 points that were **not** ion/gas tests and failed one of:

- subquestion worth **more than 3 marks**, or
- the point appears in **only one** question

Ion tests and gas tests were never removed (they stay in the main file).

`/` = acceptable alternatives on the same mark.

---

## A. Subquestions worth 4+ marks

### Titration salt preparation (4-mark write-ups)

- use **ammonia** / **ammonium carbonate** (or the named alkali + acid)
- **titration** / add acid or alkali via a **burette**
- note the volume / find the **reacting volume** / find the **end-point**
- **repeat without indicator** (same volumes)
- (alternative credited) heat the neutralised solution with carbon and then filter

### Soluble salt from acid + excess base (4-mark write-up)

- use the named **acid**
- **excess base** (added to **warm** acid)
- mixture **filtered** (filtrate collected)
- filtrate **partially evaporated** and left to **crystallise** / heated until **saturated** then left to form crystals

## B. Paper 3 planning (always 6 marks)

Marks from **named sections**. Missing a whole section caps the total. Diagrams score only if apparatus is **labelled**.

### How the 6 marks are bundled

- **1 from each section + extra marks** from any section
- 1 section missing → max 5; 2 missing → max 4; etc.

### Apparatus they credit

- named **container**: beaker / conical flask / boiling tube / test-tube / crucible
- **balance**
- **Bunsen** / water bath / electric heater / melting-point apparatus
- **gas syringe** or measuring cylinder / burette **over water**
- **burette** / **volumetric pipette**
- **filter funnel and filter paper**
- **thermometer**
- **measuring cylinder**

### Method / measurements they credit

- weigh empty container / tare the balance
- add a **known mass** / known volume
- **heat** / add **excess** acid or water
- **filter**
- **reweigh** or measure **volume of gas** / count bubbles / time for limewater to go milky
- **bung / cork** on a gas flask
- wait until **no more effervescence** / volume stops changing / **constant mass** / no solid remains

### Accuracy / control they credit

- **same mass** / volume / surface area / temperature / time of heating
- **excess** of one reagent
- **reheat and reweigh to constant mass**
- **repeat and average** / reliability / reproducibility / identify **anomalies**
- divided flask / tube of acid on a string / bung on **quickly** so **gas is not lost**
- **wash and dry** a solid residue before weighing

### Use of results they credit

- `% = (mass lost / original mass) × 100`
- `% copper = mass of copper / mass of alloy × 100`
- sample that produces **more gas / loses more mass** contains more X
- **greater temperature change per gram** means more thermal energy per gram
- **measure (and compare) the melting point** of a residue to test **purity**

### Standard 6-mark plans (2010–2025)

**Heating a solid / % mass lost**

- named container → weigh → add sample → heat → reweigh → **constant mass** → `% = (initial − final) / initial × 100` → repeat and average

**Gas from carbonate + acid**

- flask + bung + gas syringe (or cylinder over water)
- known mass of carbonate, **excess acid**
- method to avoid gas loss
- wait until bubbling / volume stops
- repeat and average volume

**Soluble salt from oxide / carbonate + acid**

- named container
- **excess** solid / acid until **effervescence stops**
- **filter**
- evaporate filtrate to **crystallisation point** / until **half the water evaporated** / until **crystals start to form**
- **cool**
- dry crystals

**Insoluble salt + soluble salt from a mixture**

- add named acid
- **filter**: insoluble salt = **washed and dried residue**
- crystallise **filtrate** for the soluble salt
- purity of residue by **melting point**

**Titration salt (e.g. ammonium sulfate)**

- burette or pipette
- flask + **indicator** (methyl orange)
- to **colour change / end-point**
- repeat **without indicator**, same volumes
- evaporate to crystallisation point, cool, dry

**Compare two samples (e.g. baking powder stored for different times)**

- same mass of each
- add water / heat
- measure gas volume or mass loss
- more gas / more mass lost → stored for **less time** (or the reverse they ask)

## C. One-off Paper 2 points (≤3 marks, only one question)

### Purity from boiling point of water

- if boiling point is **above 100 °C** the water is **impure**

### Extra crystallisation wording that did not repeat

- wash crystals with **organic solvent** / **cold water**
- dry in a **drying oven** / **paper towel**

### Extra titration wording that did not repeat

- **why no indicator** on the second run: so the salt is **not contaminated** by indicator
- **white tile**: so the **colour change / end-point can be seen more accurately**

## D. One-off Paper 3 written points (not ion/gas, ≤3 marks)

Skip practical readings. These explanation marks appeared once:

- use a **more precise thermometer** (e.g. 0.1 °C)
- measure the volume with a **burette** (instead of a measuring cylinder)
- check the **initial temperature** of both solutions is the same
- **repeat** experiments (as an improvement, when not already asked)

## E. Apparatus / method detail that did not repeat as a write-in

These were in the first draft from syllabus or older papers; they do **not** recur as 2010–2025 ≤3-mark write-ins, so they are not in the main file:

- read volume at the **bottom of the meniscus**, eye level
- **rinse** pipette / burette with the solution (water would **dilute** it)
- conical flask rather than a beaker so it is easier to **swirl**
- collection **over water** is unsuitable for **soluble gases** (NH₃, HCl, SO₂)
- gas syringe is better for soluble gases
- **solute / solvent / solution / saturated solution / residue / filtrate** as formal definitions
- **sharp** melting point = pure; impurity **lowers m.p.** and it melts over a **range**; impurity **raises b.p.**
- condenser: water in at the **bottom**, out at the **top**
- **do not evaporate to dryness** (loses water of crystallisation / decomposes / spits)
- named indicator colour tables (methyl orange red↔yellow, phenolphthalein colourless↔pink, thymolphthalein colourless↔blue)
- independent / dependent / control variable definitions
- locating agent **must not** be mixed into the solvent (only credited as part of longer 4-mark chromatography write-ups pre-filter; 2015–2025 Paper 2 uses “make the spots visible”)
