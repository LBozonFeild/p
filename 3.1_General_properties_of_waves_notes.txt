3.1 PAPER 2 (5054) — MARK-CARRYING LINES

DEFINITIONS
Waves transfer energy without transferring matter.
Frequency: the number of wavelengths / waves / cycles / oscillations that pass a point per unit time. Also the number generated by the source per unit time. Unit Hz. f = 1/T.
Wavelength: the distance between two consecutive identical points, e.g. consecutive crests.
Amplitude: the maximum displacement / distance from the mean (undisturbed) position.
Wavefront: a line joining points that are in phase / at the same point in the motion / a line along a crest.
Compression: particles of the medium closer together; higher density and pressure.
Rarefaction: particles further apart; lower density and pressure.
One wavelength of a longitudinal wave is compression to next compression (or rarefaction to next rarefaction). Compression to the next rarefaction is ½λ.

v = fλ
Write the equation, convert to SI, substitute, give the unit.
Frequency from a source: number of oscillations or crests divided by the time. Per minute, divide by 60. From a period: f = 1/T.
Frequency stays the same when a wave changes medium or depth.
If speed decreases, wavelength decreases. If speed increases, wavelength increases.
If only frequency of the bar is increased in a ripple tank of fixed depth, speed stays the same and wavelength decreases.

GRAPHS AND DRAWINGS
Amplitude is the vertical distance from the mean to a crest. Peak-to-trough is 2A.
On a height–time graph, period is the time for one complete wave. Equal spacing of peaks means constant frequency.
A height–time graph that starts at the mean has the first crest at T/4.
On a scale drawing of crests, measure several gaps and convert using the stated scale.

TRANSVERSE AND LONGITUDINAL
Transverse: vibration at right angles to the direction of energy transfer.
Examples: light, X-rays and other electromagnetic waves, waves on the surface of water, a wave on a rope or string, seismic S-waves.

Longitudinal: vibration parallel to the direction of energy transfer.
Examples: sound, ultrasound, seismic P-waves.

A wave on a string: particles of the string move at right angles to the travel of the wave.
Ultrasound is a longitudinal wave of higher frequency than audible sound. It needs a medium.

REFLECTION
At a plane surface, angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
Frequency, speed and wavelength stay the same.
Ripple tank: plane barrier; reflected wavefronts have the same spacing as the incident wavefronts.
Sound-reflection experiment: loudest signal when i = r.

REFRACTION
Refraction is due to a change of speed.
Water waves travel more slowly in shallow water than in deep water.
Deep to shallow: frequency the same, speed down, wavelength down, wavefronts closer together, direction bends towards the normal.
Shallow to deep: speed up, wavelength up, direction bends away from the normal.
Wavefronts stay continuous across the boundary.
One side of a wavefront reaches the boundary first, slows first, and the wavefront changes direction.
Ripple tank: glass sheet / block on the floor to make a shallow region.
Whole tank made shallower, no other change: speed down, wavelength down, frequency the same.

DIFFRACTION
Diffraction is spreading of a wave through a gap or past an edge.
Greatest through a gap when the gap is smaller than / similar to the wavelength: larger wavelength, smaller gap.
Drawing through a gap smaller than λ: semicircular / circular wavefronts of the same wavelength, centred on the gap.
At an edge: wavefronts curl into the shadow as quarter-circles centred on the corner. Same wavelength.
Smaller wavelength: less diffraction at the edge.
Hearing through an open door to a person in the shadow: diffraction. Draw curved wavefronts that reach that person.

RIPPLE TANK
Tank with water. Wooden bar or dipper on the surface, moved up and down (motor / rubber bands) to produce the wave. Lamp above and screen below, or a stroboscope, to see the crests.
Reflection: plane barrier.
Refraction: glass plate on the floor.
Diffraction: gap in a barrier, or a single edge of a block.
Energy transfer: a floating object / cork oscillates (up and down) and does not travel with the wave.
To increase frequency: move the bar up and down faster (or tighter rubber bands / lighter bar). Wavelength then decreases. Speed stays the same.
